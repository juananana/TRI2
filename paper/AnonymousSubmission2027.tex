\title{Temporal Referent Integrity:\\
A Controlled Diagnostic of Referential Resolution Timing in Tool-Using Agents}
\author{Anonymous Submission}
\affiliations{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Stable-only and one-sided evaluations can conflate a referent already committed to an entity with
a selector deliberately deferred to a later world state. We introduce \emph{temporal referent integrity}
(TRI), a controlled diagnostic for selective referent re-resolution in tool-using agents.
Matched Preserve/Reevaluate pairs hold states, selector, action, and transition fixed while
assigning opposite gold targets; changed-winner pair accuracy (PairAcc) therefore rejects both
Always-Lock and Always-Reevaluate policies. Across a 240-task cross-schema replication, Generic
controllers replace a correctly bound, still-actionable target with the refreshed selector winner
in 43/72, 38/80, and 59/79 eligible cases for Qwen, GLM, and DeepSeek. A fixed-executor consistency
check maps every such substitution to a wrong-entity write, whereas matched Compile-then-act
records none. Under equal calls and actor payloads, exposing a resolution-timing decision raises
changed-winner PairAcc for Qwen and GLM on the authored diagnostic. Transfer is model-dependent,
with a clear end-to-end gain only for GLM on source-derived pairs. Author-built public-suite
retrieval finds no strict native opportunity, but recall is uncalibrated. A post-hoc event-order rule
performs strongly on authored inventories but transfers poorly. TRI currently covers controlled,
single-refresh scalar workflows; native-workflow prevalence and open-language coverage remain
unresolved.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Consider two requests issued before the same mailbox refresh:

\begin{quote}
(1) ``Choose the highest-priority unread email now. Refresh the mailbox, then reply to it.''

(2) ``Refresh the mailbox first. Then choose the highest-priority unread email and reply.''
\end{quote}

Suppose email A is highest priority before refresh and email B afterward. The environment
transition is identical, but the correct target differs. Request (1) establishes A as an
identity commitment; request (2) deliberately leaves a description to be evaluated in the
new state. Acting on B in request (1) replaces a committed referent and produces a wrong-entity
action.

A transcript can contain both the original target and the refreshed state, yet the final actor may
apply the old selector again in the new state. Unconditionally retaining the old entity identifier
(ID) causes the complementary error on Reevaluate requests. Correct behavior depends on when the
instruction commits the action referent.

TRI separates three variables at the refresh boundary: world evidence and the current selector
winner; referential control state, which records a committed entity or a deferred selector; and
action validity, which checks whether a candidate supports the operation. A refresh can change
evidence without rewriting a commitment. Validity can reject a candidate but cannot select a
discourse-licensed replacement. Here ``authorization'' refers only to resolution timing.

Evaluations built from Stable or one-sided cases can conflate a referent already committed to an
entity with a selector deliberately deferred to a later world state. TRI makes this distinction observable through
matched opposite-gold pairs that separate selective re-resolution from unconditional retention
and recomputation.

The paper makes three contributions:
\nopagebreak[4]
\begin{itemize}
    \item We formalize bound and deferred resolution as distinct referential control states,
    separate from world evidence and action validity, and define the observations needed to
    attribute post-refresh target substitution.
    \item We construct matched Preserve/Reevaluate tasks with identical states, selectors,
    actions, and transitions. Changed-winner PairAcc distinguishes selective re-resolution from
    Always-Lock and Always-Reevaluate within this controlled policy class.
    \item We trace correctly bound targets through substitution, deterministic target-to-write
    checks, and model-facing database mutations.
    Equal-call interventions and transfer tests compare several operationalizations of an explicit
    resolution-timing decision, with model-dependent results.
\end{itemize}

The experiments follow four questions: whether the diagnostic separates referential policies,
whether substitution occurs after correct binding, whether it changes the executed entity, and
whether an explicit timing decision helps under matched calls and new expressions.

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Reference, binding, and memory.}
Discourse theories distinguish referents from changing descriptions and salience
\cite{kamp1993discourse,grosz1995centering}; coreference work recovers textual identity links
\cite{lee2017coreference,joshi2020spanbert,dasigi2019quoref}. Babu and Indukuri
\citeyearpar{babu2026entitybinding} study failures in initial tool-argument binding; TRI conditions
on correct $bind(r,S_0)$ and observes later target use. Agent memory and entity tracking retain
facts, IDs, and traces \cite{tang2026entitytracking,packer2023memgpt,sumers2024coala}.
TRI adds the control-state question of whether a retained selector output denotes a committed
referent. Temporal Blindness studies when time should trigger a new observation
\cite{cheng2026temporalblindness}, prospective-memory evaluation tests delayed intentions
\cite{liu2026pmbench}, and evolving-environment benchmarks measure belief and memory adaptation
\cite{ji2026clawarena,xu2026evoarena}. Our diagnostic isolates the propagation boundary between
updated world evidence and an action referent.

\paragraph{Post-binding persistence and resolution timing.}
Concurrent Binding Drift work studies whether a correctly bound primary carry slot remains stable,
how an initially wrong binding propagates, and whether entity locks or large language model (LLM)
re-verification reduce these errors \cite{babu2026bindingdrift}. Its workflows may also contain
derived roles; the defining endpoint is persistence of an already committed primary referent.
TRI varies the control state of the same action-target description under the same transition. The
Preserve member commits to $e=bind(r,S_0)$ before refresh, while the Reevaluate member defers the
referent and assigns gold $q(S_1)\ne q(S_0)$. Binding Drift characterizes stability after
commitment. TRI identifies whether commitment preceded the transition. These are complementary
axes, summarized in Table~\ref{tab:closest-boundary}.

\paragraph{Tool agents and executable constraints.}
Tool-use benchmarks cover application programming interfaces (APIs), web environments, and stateful interaction
\cite{schick2023toolformer,li2023apibank,qin2024toolllm,patil2023gorilla,
liu2024agentbench,lu2025toolsandbox,trivedi2024appworld,
yao2024taubench,barres2025tau2,sierra2026tau3,ruan2024toolemu}.
Process-aware graphical-interface evaluation likewise shows that final-state success can hide a
critical intermediate error \cite{yang2026probench}. The pinned suites audited here lack gold
labels for strict post-binding Preserve/Reevaluate opportunities; broader tool-use benchmarks
commonly emphasize task completion. LedgerAgent and Bounded Autonomy add typed state or action
contracts \cite{uddin2026ledgeragent,sohail2026bounded}. Such validity constraints filter
candidates, while discourse supplies the intended identity. AgentSpec and policy autoformalization compile or enforce
rules, VIGIL monitors temporal and value-flow constraints, and Cordon stages multi-step effects
before commit \cite{wang2025agentspec,mondl2026autoformalization,li2026vigil,chen2026cordon}.
These systems address executable constraints; our matched design evaluates the bound-versus-deferred
resolution contrast.
Structured generation can enforce syntax
\cite{beurer2023lmql,willard2023guided,khattab2024dspy}, while our clustered minimal pairs
follow diagnostic and contrast-set methodology \cite{ribeiro2020checklist,gardner2020contrast}.
Meta-evaluation studies discrimination across model capabilities \cite{wang2026metaeval}; TRI asks
whether a regime discriminates referential policies that can obtain the same score.
Complementary semantic conditions have similarly exposed one-sided large language model (LLM) heuristics and correction
trade-offs in aspectual natural-language inference (NLI) \cite{ma2026imperfective}; TRI applies the diagnostic logic to
post-binding control and executed actions, not lexical aspect or static entailment.

\section{Temporal Referent Integrity and Evaluation Identifiability}

Let $H$ be the instruction and interaction history before a refresh, $S_0$ and $S_1$ the states
before and after it, $r$ an action-target reference, and
$q_r:S\rightarrow E\cup\{\bot\}$ its deterministic task resolver. For each frozen task,
$q_r(S)$ returns one eligible entity, or $\bot$ when none is eligible; unresolved ties are excluded.
The formalization keeps three variables distinct: $S$ and $q_r(S)$ represent world evidence,
$C_H(r)$ represents referential commitment, and $V_a(e,S)$ represents action validity. Let
$C_H^-(r)$ and $C_H^+(r)$ denote control state immediately before and after refresh. History
induces one of two pre-refresh states:
\begin{equation}
C_H^-(r)\in\{B(e_0),U(q_r)\},\qquad e_0=q_r(S_0).
\end{equation}
$B(e_0)$ is an identity commitment resolved before refresh; $U(q_r)$ is a query intentionally
left unresolved until afterward. Updating world evidence can change $q_r(S)$ without changing a
bound commitment:
\begin{equation}
S_0\ne S_1\;\not\Rightarrow\;C_H^+(r)\ne C_H^-(r).
\end{equation}
Under Preserve, $C_H^+(r)=B(e_0)$. Under Reevaluate, the unresolved query $U(q_r)$ is evaluated
only in $S_1$, yielding $B(q_r(S_1))$. Replacing an existing $B(e)$ with a distinct $B(e')$
requires the additional discourse-sensitive decision $\Gamma_H(r,e\!\rightarrow\!e')$.

We represent each workflow with four observable events: \textsc{Observe}, \textsc{Bind},
\textsc{Refresh}, and \textsc{Write}. The target is resolved from an observed collection, a state
transition occurs, and a later entity-directed mutation consumes the target. Public-benchmark
coverage records which events and resolution-time contrasts are observable.

An identifying trace requires a binding, an update, a changed selector winner, a still-valid old
target, and a later entity-directed action. The initial ID separates binding errors from later
substitution. Matched Preserve/Reevaluate cases share one transition and require opposite targets,
which tests both unconditional policies. A state diff or target-level execution record then connects
the selected target to the resulting write. This yields three evidence levels: policy behavior,
error attribution, and executed consequence.

\paragraph{Resolution timing and action validity.}
For execution, $V_a(e,S)$ conjoins the action-schema preconditions, and invalid-target policy
$\pi:E\times S\rightarrow E\cup\{\bot\}$ returns $\bot$ or, when authorized, $q_r(S_1)$.
The authorized target is
\begin{equation}
T^*=\begin{cases}
e_0, & C_H^-(r)=B(e_0)\ \land\ V_a(e_0,S_1),\\
q_r(S_1), & C_H^-(r)=U(q_r),\\
\pi(e_0,S_1), & C_H^-(r)=B(e_0)\ \land\ \neg V_a(e_0,S_1).
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
The first two branches define the actionable TRI core. The third is a separate execution policy:
when the committed target is invalid, a system may reject, clarify, or reselect if the instruction
permits it. Referential identity and fallback choice are therefore scored separately. Controllers
operationalize the timing decision in different ways; one representation used below is
$L=(m,e,q,V_a,\pi)$. Action validity accepts or rejects a candidate but supplies no replacement
identity.

\paragraph{Benchmark-design observation.}
Consider a Preserve/Reevaluate minimal pair with identical $S_0$, $S_1$, $q$, and $a$, where
$e=q(S_0)$, $e'=q(S_1)$, and $e\ne e'$. The authorized targets are $e$ and $e'$, respectively.
For exact-target observations within the policy class \{Always-Lock, Always-Reevaluate,
Selective\}, discrimination requires one changed Preserve case and one changed Reevaluate case.
Either row alone leaves Selective observationally equivalent to one unconditional policy. The
opposite-gold pair separates all three and is the smallest such set in this restricted class. This
is a benchmark-design condition, not a general result for arbitrary stochastic controllers; the
short argument is supplementary.

More generally, an evaluation can identify a policy only when its scored tasks separate that
policy from relevant alternatives. Stable tasks make lock and reevaluation observationally
equivalent, while a one-sided changed-winner test can reward one unconditional extreme. The
crossed matched regime breaks both equivalences. For a matched pair, PairAcc is one
only when both members are correct (Table~\ref{tab:policy-identifiability}).

\section{Diagnostic Policies and Controller Probes}

The Generic Structured Ledger controller (Generic) retains the instruction and selected ID without
encoding resolution mode. The comparison controllers are mechanism probes for explicit
resolution-timing decisions. Compile-then-act (CTA) emits binding time, selector, and a bound ID
before refresh without a deterministic gate. Lifecycle records the same timing decision together
with validity and fallback fields; its actor-mediated and gated variants test soft and hard use of
that record. Both compilers use discourse order: ``identify, refresh, act on it'' preserves a
resolved identity, while ``refresh before deciding'' evaluates the selector in $S_1$. The complete
record schema and gate branches are supplementary.

The anonymous Code and Data Supplement contains prompts, runner interfaces, inventories, raw
outputs, and executable reports. Controllers receive the instruction's natural-language selector.
Gold targets, normalized selector fields, and pre-/post-refresh winner IDs are withheld.

For information parity, compilers see the instruction, $S_0$, action schema, and planned refresh. Generic retains the
instruction, selected ID, snapshot, natural-language selector,
action, and preconditions for its second-call actor. CTA, Lifecycle-Actor, and Generic each use
two calls; Lifecycle-Gated skips the actor for a valid Preserve branch, making the primary package
comparison call-asymmetric. Complete settings are in the Code and Data Supplement.
These probes test operationalizations of the same timing variable. The evidence supports no unique
requirement for lifecycle typing, pre-refresh compilation, or hard gating.

\section{Experimental Setup}

We evaluate the resolution-time distinction using the \PrimaryDiagnostic{},
\NewSchemaReplication{}, and supplementary \GuardedExtension{}.

\paragraph{Controlled task construction.}
Each domain specification defines an entity table, a natural-language selector, its ranking or
Boolean criterion, an action, and explicit action preconditions. The generator first evaluates
the selector on $S_0$ to obtain $e_0$, applies a controlled transition, and evaluates it on
$S_1$ to obtain $e_1$. A language template determines whether the action target is $e_0$,
$e_1$, $\bot$, or a conditionally guarded choice. Paired tasks share the domain, both states,
selector, action, and schema; only discourse order and anaphoric language change. This prevents
domain difficulty or target frequency from explaining the preserve/reevaluate contrast.

\paragraph{Workflow task generation.}
The generator organizes selector questions into a four-event workflow: \textsc{Observe} a
user-visible collection, \textsc{Bind} one stable ID, apply an
exogenous or synchronization-style \textsc{Refresh}, and execute a target-specific \textsc{Write}.
Domain templates fill these slots with ordinary operations (reply to a message, comment on a
reminder, update a project or expense, change an inventory record, or deploy a selected artifact).
The same-role replacement requirement is added when the application exposes both a stable ID
and a ranking or eligibility selector. This yields a crossed factorial 2$\times$2 core
(Preserve/Reevaluate $\times$ Stable/changed-winner), while Remove, Invalidate, and name collision are
orthogonal stressors. The coverage audit records which parts of this structure appear in public
suites; deployment frequency requires natural workflow data.

The crossed core combines Preserve/Reevaluate with an unchanged or changed selector winner.
\emph{Stable} controls expose overreaction to refresh; \emph{changed-winner} and \emph{name collision} create
opposite-target pairs while retaining valid stable IDs. \emph{Remove} and \emph{Invalidate} form a
separate fallback-policy slice; the complete transition taxonomy is supplementary.

\paragraph{Blind human construct validation.}
One adult volunteer produced natural-language rewrites of 50 sampled scalar instructions; three other adults,
uninvolved in TRI design, independently labeled 100 randomized original/rewrite items using
opaque IDs without condition metadata or gold. All four volunteers provided informed consent; no
sensitive personal information was collected, released responses were de-identified, compensation,
where provided, was non-contingent, and no formal IRB or exemption determination was obtained. We report
agreement, majority--gold alignment, and consensus sensitivity only for the single-target scalar core.
Referent identity is the TRI construct; whether an invalid action should Reject, Clarify, or select
another entity is a separate execution-policy judgment.

The frozen error taxonomy separates temporal rebinding, premature binding, invalid processing,
unnecessary rejection, and selector grounding; definitions are supplementary.

\paragraph{Direct-neighbor baseline.}
Generic Structured Ledger stores the initial ID, entity snapshot, selector, action, and
preconditions but omits reference mode and fallback semantics; its actor still receives the
original instruction and refreshed state. Frozen CTA is rerun unchanged as a neighboring
pre-refresh commitment baseline. The full component comparison, including the untyped planner,
appears in Supplementary Material and compares implementations of the timing decision on scalar tasks.

For a direct policy contrast, we also evaluate two deterministic extremes without model calls.
Always-Lock + validity retains the $S_0$ ID and rejects it when the refreshed action
preconditions fail; Always-Reevaluate discards the binding and applies $q$ to $S_1$. These are
deterministic realizations of the two unconditional extremes. On the frozen
160-task inventory both reach 96/160 (60.0\%), but their mode slices are complementary:
Always-Lock reaches 80/80 anchored and 16/80 dynamic, whereas Always-Reevaluate reaches
16/80 anchored and 80/80 dynamic. Their complementary slices expose the selective re-resolution
requirement.

\paragraph{Model-facing SQLite trajectories.}
The 40-task SQLite subset covers all templates, domains, modes, and updates. An action-validating
mutation tool records final state, wrong writes, invalid attempts, and collateral changes. This
controlled execution test connects selected targets to database mutations.

\paragraph{Models, inference, and auditing.}

\section{Results}

\subsection{Diagnostic Discrimination}
Always-Lock and Always-Reevaluate pass Stable cases and opposite one-sided changed-winner slices,
yet both obtain zero PairAcc on complete changed pairs. A zero-API policy audit confirms the
selection problem: all 15 maximizer sets under Stable-only or one-sided scoring contain a
zero-PairAcc policy, with worst-case PairAcc regret of 100 points. Aggregate end-to-end (E2E)
scoring selects a PairAcc-optimal candidate in all five candidate sets. Figure~\ref{fig:resolution-policy-phase-space}
shows how the matched score separates the unconditional policies from selective re-resolution.
Thus PairAcc reveals joint policy behavior and symmetric errors in the current study; we do not
observe a controller-ranking reversal under aggregate E2E scoring.

The actionable referential core contains 128 tasks. Generic reaches 95/128 (74.2\%) for Qwen and
93/128 (72.7\%) for GLM; CTA reaches 126/128 (98.4\%) and 127/128 (99.2\%), and Lifecycle-Gated
reaches 125/128 (97.7\%) and 128/128 (100.0\%). The remaining 32 anchored remove/invalidate rows
use an author-specified \textsc{Reject} gold and form a separate fallback-policy slice. Generic,
CTA, and Gated obtain 8/32 and 22/32, 26/32 and 27/32, and 32/32 for Qwen and GLM, respectively.

The construct check supports the same separation. Across 100 blinded original and rewritten items,
Fleiss' $\kappa=.708$, Krippendorff's $\alpha=.709$, and majority--gold agreement is 86\%
(91.5\% among 94 determinate items). Agreement falls to 55\% majority--gold and 25\% unanimity for
action-invalid \textsc{Reject}. Against 46 determinate original-item majorities, Generic/CTA reach
69.6/91.3\% for Qwen and 73.9/89.1\% for GLM. We base the referential claim on the
actionable core and treat rejection, clarification, and authorized reselection as execution policy.

\subsection{Substitution After Correct Binding}
We next condition on a correct observable initial ID, a completed refresh, a distinct new selector
winner, and an old target that remains present and action-valid. In the primary diagnostic, Qwen
Generic substitutes on 15/16 anchored flips and 14/16 name collisions; GLM substitutes on 3/16 and
7/16. Stable controls show no such concentration.

The 240-task \NewSchemaReplication{} extends this test to ten schemas. Generic selects the refreshed
winner in 43/72 Qwen, 38/80 GLM, and 59/79 DeepSeek opportunities. CTA records zero substitutions
among 71, 70, and 70 eligible cases. On shared-eligible rows, where both controllers bind correctly,
the comparisons are 41/66 versus 0/66, 30/70 versus 0/70, and 50/69 versus 0/69
(Figure~\ref{fig:shared-eligible-target-flow}). The conditioning isolates replacement of a correct
commitment from initial binding and target-validity errors.

\subsection{Target-to-Write Verification}
A deterministic fixed-executor consistency check turns all 43, 38, and 59 Generic substitutions
into wrong-entity writes.
Their cluster-bootstrap intervals are [46.5,72.4], [35.0,61.3], and [62.5,86.1] percent. Initial-ID,
missing-ID, action-invalid, API, and parse failures are excluded from this conditional denominator;
Stable errors and non-core writes remain separate. Figure~\ref{fig:wrong-write-decomposition}
reports the complete decomposition.

The model-facing 40-task SQLite subset supplies separate tool-loop evidence. Generic reaches 27/40
final states with 13 wrong writes for Qwen and 26/40 with eight wrong writes for GLM. Under the
strict actionable-core conditions, refreshed-winner writes occur in 8/8 Qwen and 6/8 GLM
opportunities, versus 0/4 Stable controls for each model. In one trace, Qwen records
\texttt{REM-1A} before refresh and then sends \texttt{REM-1B} to the mutation tool. The remaining
five Qwen and two GLM wrong writes arise in the fallback-policy slice. These trajectories locate
the failure between binding and action; the fixed replay only verifies target-to-write consistency.

\subsection{Decision Visibility and Transfer}
The Evidence Map records experimental chronology; this section uses the shorter dataset names.
The full-diagnostic matched-call test compares History-only and Decision-visible actors with equal
calls, base payloads, states, and tool schemas. The actor-visible decision block is the manipulated
field under matched calls and base payloads; the compiler representation remains part of the probe.

\paragraph{Authored diagnostic.}
On 32 actionable changed pairs, PairAcc rises from 5/32 to 13/32 for Qwen (+25.0 points
[6.3,46.2]) and from 8/32 to 25/32 for GLM (+53.1 [28.6,77.8]). Actionable E2E changes from
100/128 to 106/128 and from 102/128 to 120/128. Substitution among correctly bound actionable
Preserve cases falls from 22/28 to 13/28 and from 16/25 to 0/25. All 320 task-model rows complete,
comprising 640 actor outputs and 320 compiler outputs, without API or parse failure.

Offline Decision-enforced raises Qwen PairAcc to 24/32, with 18 repairs and eight row-level harms.
GLM remains at 25/32; enforcement repairs four Reject rows and harms none. The visibility result is
consistent across the two authored models, while hard enforcement has mixed effects.

\paragraph{Human rewrites.}
Against 48 determinate rewrite majorities, CTA reaches 89.6\% for Qwen and 93.8\% for GLM. In the

to 35/48. GLM improves from 31/40 to 39/40 and from 33/48 to 41/48. Only three actionable changed
pairs are complete: Qwen changes from 0/3 to 1/3 and GLM from 1/3 to 3/3, with wide cluster
intervals. These one-volunteer rewrites test authored task semantics, not open-language validation.

\paragraph{Alternative operationalizations.}
The primary Qwen package comparison and GLM replication are call-asymmetric mechanism probes.
Lifecycle-Gated improves over Generic on both, while CTA and a full-history timing reminder show
that lifecycle typing and hard gating are not unique implementations. Full results and intervals
are supplementary.

The initial no-LLM event-order rule scores 60.6\% on \PrimaryDiagnostic{}. Rule*, written after
error inspection, reaches 92.5\% on \PrimaryDiagnostic{}, 96.0\% on rewrites, and 91.7\% on
\NewSchemaReplication{}. This strong authored-inventory result shows that the diagnostic contains
usable event-order cues. On the source-derived pairs, the frozen rule reaches 15/60 exact targets
and 2/30 PairAcc; timestamps, extra numeric fields, and unfamiliar ranking expressions expose
parser and selector-grounding errors. The residual and component analyses are supplementary.
Together they characterize typing, compilation, reminders, rules, and gates as alternative
implementations of the same timing decision.

\paragraph{Persistence-policy adaptations.}
We author-adapt the Binding Drift entity-lock semantics and self-reverification prompt to all 240
actionable cross-schema tasks \cite{babu2026bindingdrift}. Entity lock favors Preserve and
self-reverification favors Reevaluate, characterizing the two persistence policies. The adapted
verifier receives the instruction and refreshed candidates without $S_0$ or the pre-refresh bound
ID, so this is not an official reproduction or an information-matched performance comparison.
Detailed mode and pair counts are supplementary.

\paragraph{Boundary analyses.}
External evidence is mixed. A 24-task ToolSandbox-style pilot finds GLM substitution in 3/6
eligible changed-winner cases versus 0/2 Stable cases, while Qwen is 0/6. Its four-condition,
96-task extension finds zero conditional substitutions in 64--87 eligible opportunities. Across 20 STATE-Bench and
AgentDojo clusters \cite{microsoft2026statebench,debenedetti2024agentdojo}, Qwen ordinary history
substitutes in 2/7 eligible AgentDojo Preserve/Changed rows versus 0/7 matched Stable rows; the other
repository-model cells are zero and execution accuracy has no consistent winner.

Under an author-built checklist and retrieval procedure, the public-suite audit identifies no
strict native opportunity in pinned ToolSandbox (129 families), AppWorld (244 families), or
$\tau^3$-Bench (2,449 tasks; 10,832 trajectories). A
structural scan of API-Bank, BFCL, and ToolTalk also finds zero. The checklist accepts all 30
injected strict positives and excludes all 30 one-condition-missing hard negatives, but natural
retrieval recall has not been independently calibrated.

Structural stress tests remain mixed. A two-refresh Qwen test favors Generic over scalar Lifecycle
(32/40 vs. 28/40), while held-out role indexing improves 35/40 to 39/40. GLM role ITT is 37/40
versus 40/40 scalar because of three transport failures and ties after recovery. These results set
the current scope at single-refresh scalar mutations.

\section{Discussion}

The evidence chain starts with a correct observable binding and ends with a target-level state
change. Generic often carries the initial ID through refresh, recomputes its selector on $S_1$, and
sends the new winner to the mutation tool. PairAcc exposes this selective-policy failure, while
conditional substitution distinguishes it from initial binding and grounding errors. Fixed replay
checks whether the selected target produces the corresponding write; SQLite trajectories provide
the model-facing execution evidence.

CTA, lifecycle records, timing reminders, and deterministic rules all operationalize a
resolution-timing decision. Their relative performance supports no unique controller design.
Rule* is especially informative: event order plus benchmark-aware lexical coverage is sufficient
for high accuracy on the authored inventories, while source-derived ranking expressions introduce
parser and selector-grounding failures. The benchmark can therefore be solved by a simple
executable rule when the expression inventory is covered; open wording adds a separate
implementation challenge.

\paragraph{Evidence scope.}
The Evidence Map separates the primary Qwen package run from replications, matched-call tests,
audits, and post-hoc analyses. The primary package comparison measures an end-to-end controller
contrast. The equal-call experiments isolate visibility of the timing decision, and the rule and
component studies examine alternative implementations. This organization supports a diagnostic
claim about selective re-resolution, with architecture ranking outside its scope.

External evidence is mixed. Eligible low-intervention runs show no conditional substitutions, the
audited suites contain no strict native cases, and retrieval recall has not been independently
calibrated. The source-derived matched pairs supply controlled transfer evidence, with a clear E2E
gain only for GLM. A broader benchmark will need observable initial bindings, opposite-gold
transitions, and target-level execution records in naturally occurring workflows.

\section{Limitations}

The controlled diagnostic uses single-refresh scalar tasks and simplifies ambiguity, repair,
partial observability, identity migration, and composition. \NewSchemaReplication{} varies schemas
and states while retaining authored language templates. Volunteer rewrites preserve authored task
semantics, and only three actionable changed pairs are complete. A model-authored language audit
fails its semantic gate because two judges accept no complete pair. Open-language generalization
therefore remains unresolved.

Referent identity is better supported than fallback policy: agreement is weak when the old target
is action-invalid, and Reject, Clarify, and authorized reselection may all be reasonable execution
choices. Multiple refreshes and referent roles also require richer control state; the two-refresh
results show that the scalar record does not compose automatically.

The public-suite and low-intervention results may reflect low prevalence, benchmark coverage, or
retrieval misses. Current evidence cannot separate these explanations. The primary package
comparison is call-asymmetric, whereas the equal-call studies supply the initial ID and isolate
decision visibility. Temperature-zero repeats vary at task level, and provider weights lack
immutable revision identifiers.

\section{Conclusion}

TRI makes selective referent re-resolution identifiable by pairing the same transition with bound
and deferred action references. Changed-winner PairAcc rejects both unconditional target policies,
and the execution trace connects substitution after correct binding to wrong-entity writes.
Exposing an explicit timing decision helps Qwen and GLM on the authored matched-call test; transfer
is model-dependent, with a clear source-derived E2E gain only for GLM. Rule*, hard enforcement, and
composition results show that the same control decision can be represented in several ways and
limit the algorithmic novelty claim. The current evidence covers controlled single-refresh scalar workflows.
Native-workflow frequency and general runtime behavior remain unmeasured; open-language coverage remains open.
